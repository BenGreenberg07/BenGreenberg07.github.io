Autonomous Blimp Drone

Aerial Swarms Lab, Lehigh University · 2026

codepaper

An autonomous indoor blimp for rehabilitation research. Lift is the constraint: every gram of structure trades directly against sensing, wiring, and battery. I designed the airframe around an ESP32 flight board and IMU, then wrote the control firmware in C, flown over an ESP-NOW radio bridge and tracked with OptiTrack motion capture. Pushed 2.23 m off its target, it flies itself back.

Cantilever Truss: Design, Build, and Load Test

Swarthmore College, ENGR 006 · April 2026

An aluminum cantilever truss scored on minimum weight and deflection under a 1,000 lb point load, with a 30 in minimum span, 18 in height limit, and 30° minimum member angle. I sized 14 members across 9 joints (hollow square tube in compression, flat bar in tension). We skipped the lightest simulated geometry because it needed tighter angles than we could assemble accurately, trading weight for buildability.

It held the load but deflected 0.390 in against 0.121 in predicted; my post-test analysis traced the gap to out-of-plane misalignment from joints placed by eye.

6.45 lb truss 1,000 lb carried 14 members · 9 joints

Tensile Testing and Material Selection

Swarthmore College, ENGR 006 · May 2026

Characterized Aluminum 6061 against C1018 steel on an Instron universal testing system to pick a material for the department truss program. I wrote the MATLAB that turned raw force and displacement into engineering stress-strain curves. Steel was far stronger, but I recommended aluminum: once density was accounted for it won on cost per unit volume, machinability, and being light enough for students to handle safely.

UTS 297.8 vs 754.5 MPa $13.1k vs $23k per unit volume

Custom End Attachment for a Haptic Research Device

Swarthmore College, neuromuscular hand-control lab · 2025 – present

code

The stock PHANToM end effector forced a whole-hand grip, making the finger-specific strength measurements the study needed impossible. I designed and printed an attachment for one- or two-finger interaction, sized around the hand geometry and grip strength of post-stroke patients, and wrote C++ force-feedback and position-tracking software on the OpenHaptics SDK. It is now central to the lab's patient-testing protocol.
